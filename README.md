# Shush 🤫

> **shush** /ʃʊʃ/ _verb_  
> To urge or request silence.

Shush is a typewriter in a browser tab. It gives you a sheet of paper and a line to type on, and very little else. It's made for putting thoughts on paper in quiet, whether that's a poem, a letter, or an idea that won't wait.

It borrows the mechanics of a typewriter, and with them the feel of writing on one. On a typewriter the letters always strike the same spot; it is the paper that moves. The carriage moves the paper across as you type and brings it back at the end of each line, and a roller, the platen, feeds it up one line at a time. A knob on the platen winds the paper back or forward. Here it's the same: the letters stay put and the page moves.

The page is a sheet of A4 typed in pica, the classic typewriter size: 62 characters to a line and 38 lines to a page, the same on screen and in print. Spaces and line breaks are kept exactly as typed and marked faintly on the page. When you're done, take the paper out to read it as pages, print it, or save it as plain text.

Beyond typing, there is a small set of tools: bold, underline, strikethrough, a red ribbon and a highlighter; tab stops, with a center stop; and moving through the text by letter, word, line or paragraph.

Shush is free and open to anyone, with nothing to sign up for. There is one sheet at a time, and it never leaves the computer it was written on.
